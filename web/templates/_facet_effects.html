About the flags

replicate level (donor)
Tested with one profile per replicate (donor, animal or sample). The replicate, not the cell, is the unit.
cell-level, descriptive only
Too few replicates for a test (fewer than 3 in a group, or no replicate column). The numbers describe cells; there is no p or q value because cells of one donor are not independent.
descriptive: below the p-value floor (donors need 5 per group)
The unit was fitted, but with fewer replicates than the p-value floor (5 donors per group, 10 for a trend such as Braak or age; 3 animals per group, 6 for a trend). The estimate is shown without p, q or interval, and the unit is not counted towards replication.
small sample
Above the floor but with fewer than 5 replicates per group (10 for a trend): tested and counted, with little power. The group line says how many of the cohorts counted in m have fewer than 20 donors.
inflated, lambda 2.3
Many genes shift together (lambda above 1.3), which usually points to a batch, depth or composition difference rather than biology.
covariates dropped (1)
A covariate (sex, age, post-mortem interval, batch) was left out to keep the test or at least 4 residual degrees of freedom; the tooltip names it.
control: humanised-App host
What the control animals of an App NL-G-F comparison are.
persists after pathology adjustment
For the age facet: the gene's age effect is still there when Braak stage (else pathologic AD) is in the model.
provisional
What counts as control still differs between studies, so disease results may change.
composition
The share of this cell type among a donor's cells changes with the facet (p < 0.05, uncorrected). The effect may reflect which cells were captured. A change in the mix of states inside the cell type is not flagged.
batch confounded
The facet and batch cannot be separated, so batch was left out of the model.
pooled
Several files of one cohort were combined per donor into one unit, so the cohort counts once.
donor overlap not checked
Nobody has verified whether this cohort shares donors with another one. Shared donors make cohorts agree for the wrong reason.
reanalysis, not counted
A reanalysis of cells another dataset serves: shown, never counted.

Exploratory. Effects are tested per donor within one cell type. How to read this

{% call doc_block('About these effects', id='fx-about') %}

{% if scope == 'dataset' %}Each tested facet is compared within one cell type of this dataset.{% else %}Each row is one dataset and cell type, tested on its own.{% endif %} The cells of one donor in that cell type are added up into one profile per donor (a pseudobulk), and genes are compared between donors with limma-voom, adjusting for sex, age and post-mortem interval where these are known. The donor, not the cell, is the replicate unit. Module scores are tested with a linear model and {% if n_permutations %}{{ "{:,}".format(n_permutations) }} label permutations{% else %}label permutations (10,000 by default; each row's model line gives its own number){% endif %}.

Across cohorts, a gene replicates when the same direction is seen in at least 2 cohorts. The r-value is a false discovery rate for the whole list of genes, not a probability for one gene, and it says nothing about effect size; the effect filter (at least a 10% change in 2 cohorts) is shown separately. Each cohort counts once, through one anchor unit, so datasets that share donors do not count twice.

No hit means "not detected with this many donors", not "no effect". "AD" means pathologic AD (Braak and CERAD), not a clinical diagnosis, and only cohorts with a pathology-based definition are combined. A change of a whole cell type can also be a change in the mix of cell states within it. Terms are explained in the glossary.

  • Unit of analysis. The replicate (donor for human cohorts, host animal for mouse and xenograft data). Raw counts are summed per replicate within one cell type (at least 10 cells per replicate).
  • Genes. limma-voom with quality weights and robust moderation; model expression ~ facet + sex + age + pmi [+ batch], covariates only when known for enough donors. q values are Benjamini-Hochberg within one dataset, cell type and facet. A second test, limma treat, asks whether the change exceeds a 10% minimum; its q is shown as q (≥10% change).
  • Modules. A per-donor score, an ordinary linear model per module, and permutations of the facet labels across donors (10,000 by default). q_param and q_perm are Benjamini-Hochberg within one dataset, cell type, facet, method and score source.
  • Contrasts. Sex m vs f, pathologic AD vs control, APOE e4 carrier vs non-carrier, App NL-G-F vs control (host animals of xenografts); Braak and age are straight-line trends (per stage, per decade of age, donors from 60 years). Age is tested twice: not adjusted for pathology, and adjusted for it (Braak stage, else pathologic AD). Braak stage III or higher against lower and the number of e4 alleles are sensitivity analyses. With fewer replicates than the p-value floor (5 donors per group, 10 for a trend; 3 and 6 animals) a row is descriptive: estimate only, no p, q or interval, not counted.
  • Replication. One anchor unit per cohort, cell type group and scope. A partial conjunction test (Benjamini and Heller 2008, Simes) of "same direction in at least 2 cohorts" gives an r-value per gene, adjusted by Benjamini-Hochberg over the genes of the group; replicated means r < 0.05. The effect filter (at least 10% in 2 cohorts), the second tier "replicated with at least a 10% change" (the same test on the treat p values) and the count k of cohorts individually at q < 0.05 are shown beside it. Results are counted within one scope (human post-mortem, or human cells in mouse hosts); scopes are never mixed.
  • Provenance. Each row states its own model, covariates and run under "details". Rows are separate tests; units of one cohort are not summed.

Exploratory. How {{ gene }} differs with each facet, tested per donor within one cell type. How to read this

{% call doc_block('About these effects', id='gene-fx-about') %}

Each dataset and cell type is tested on its own, with one profile per donor (a pseudobulk). One line per cell type group says whether {{ gene }} replicates across cohorts: the same direction in at least 2 cohorts, counted through one anchor unit per cohort. Open a line for its anchors and datasets.

The r-value is a false discovery rate for the whole list of replicated genes, not a probability for {{ gene }}, and it does not say how large the change is. A change in a whole cell type can also be a change in the mix of cell states within it. "Disease" means pathologic AD (Braak and CERAD), and only pathology-based definitions are combined. Terms are explained in the glossary.

Scopes. Human post-mortem tissue and human cells grafted into mouse hosts are analysed and counted separately and never mixed: in a xenograft the replicate is the host animal, and the gene is the human gene in a mouse brain. Pick the scope first, then the facet. The age facet appears twice, not adjusted and adjusted for pathology; Braak stage III or higher and the number of e4 alleles are sensitivity analyses of the Braak trend and the carrier contrast (glossary).
