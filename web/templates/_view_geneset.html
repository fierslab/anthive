{% call section_help('1. Module score', 'vws-p1-help') %}

This panel is descriptive: for each cell type it shows how high {{ name }} ranks among the gene sets of the same dataset, not whether anything changes. Click a row to list its datasets.

Rank among gene sets

A mean module score cannot be compared between datasets, because every dataset has its own scale, depth and background. So the bar shows where the mean score of {{ name }} falls among ALL gene sets scored on the same cells of the same dataset, as "top x%" (a longer bar means a higher mean score than more of the other sets; "of N" is the number of other sets compared). In a cell type row the value is the mean over its datasets, in an opened row the dotted line marks that mean. Where gene sets are scored only on the cells of their own cell type, a set is compared with the sets scored on those cells (sets that apply to every cell are included), and a set that covers several cell types gets the cell-weighted mean of its ranks per cell type.

The ranking uses the scanpy score, which subtracts the mean of a matched control set of genes, so scores of different sets in one dataset are comparable. UCell is not used here: it depends on the number of genes in the set. The rank compares gene sets within one dataset only and says nothing about how the same set scores in another dataset. It needs at least 10 other sets scored on at least 20 cells, otherwise it reads n/a. The first visit of a dataset can take a moment while its scores are read.

Cell-to-cell SD

How much the score varies between cells within a dataset (median over the datasets of the row). Means and standard deviations are taken over the cells of one dataset: they describe the cells and say nothing about differences between donors.

Which datasets

Scores exist only in the datasets where the set was scored in advance. The facet results in the next tab come from donor-level scores of the datasets they were tested in, which can be more datasets.

Rank and cell type

A set is scored on the cells of its own cell type, so in a cell type other than the one it was built for it ranks low by construction. Read the rank in the cell type the set belongs to.

Exploratory. The module result tests the mean score of the whole gene set per donor (per host mouse in the xenograft block) against each facet, separately in every cohort, and combines the cohorts with the same partial conjunction test as for single genes (r-value below 0.05, Benjamini-Hochberg over the modules of one family).

Ranked shifts

One line per facet and cell type with a module result, best first, within one system (post-mortem and xenograft are never mixed). The order is the partial-conjunction p value before adjustment: the r-value saturates at 1 for most pairs and would tie. Each line gives the median effect over the counted cohorts with the smallest to largest cohort effect as a whisker, the direction in words, how many cohorts point the same way and how many are significant on their own, the r-value, and the rank of this gene set among all gene sets tested for the same facet and cell type. The first five are shown, the rest are folded.

How to read the matrix

A cell is the module result only: k/m is the number of cohorts significant on their own out of the cohorts tested, grey is not replicated, teal is replicated (same direction in at least 2 cohorts), the arrow gives the direction, a dash means not tested. There is no strict tier for sets. The effect filter for modules is a standardised effect of at least 0.2 standard deviations in 2 cohorts. Member genes are not on the cells: the line "Member genes replicating" under the matrix counts, per facet and cell type, the member genes that replicate on their own (r-value below 0.05) out of those tested in at least 2 cohorts. Genes on the X or Y chromosome are left out for the sex facet.

The effect bar

The bar runs from a zero line on a fixed scale for its unit (standard deviations of the donor-level score: two-group contrasts plus or minus 1, per Braak stage and per decade plus or minus 0.3, per e4 allele plus or minus 0.5). An arrowhead means the value is beyond the scale. Warm is up, cool is down, grey is not counted. A filled bar means the cohort on its own has q below 0.05, an open bar means it does not. In the ranked shifts the bar is the median over the counted cohorts and is filled when the result replicates.

Pseudobulk and cell mean

Where counts exist, the result of the donor-level pseudobulk of the member genes (B) is shown; otherwise the mean of the per-cell scores per donor (A), and the opened cell says which. A warning marks cohorts where A and B disagree in a way that matters (one is replicated, or both reach 0.2 SD with opposite signs). A flat module result can hide member genes that replicate on their own, and the other way round.

Caveats shown under a matrix

  • Composition. A change of a whole cell type can be a shift in the mix of cell states inside it (composition), not a change in every cell.
  • One pathology axis. Braak stage and pathologic AD are one pathology axis, so where both replicate it is one finding.
  • Systems. The post-mortem and the xenograft system are analysed separately and never combined (scopes). In the xenograft block all cohorts come from one lab pipeline.

The opened cell

A click on a cell lists the datasets behind it, one row per dataset: the cohort, the number of donors (test vs reference for two-group facets, host mice in the xenograft block), the effect and the adjusted p. The effect is the standardised effect of the mean score of the set in that dataset, in standard deviations of the donor-level score: test minus reference for two-group facets, per Braak stage, per decade of age or per e4 allele for trend facets. The adjusted p is the q of that dataset on its own (permutation q where it is used, else the parametric q), not the replication result. A row marked "not counted" is shown but does not take part in k/m. Donor plots stay reachable from the dataset page.
