This page answers one question: which genes and gene sets change with {{ facet_label }}, and where is that finding repeated? A facet is a donor-level property defined the same way in every dataset, so an effect is a difference between donors (between host mice in the xenograft scope), not between cells.

A gene counts when the same direction appears in at least 2 cohorts according to a partial conjunction test at 5 percent FDR (r-value) and the change is at least 10 percent in 2 cohorts (effect filter, a condition on size, not error control). The strict subset also passes the minimum-effect test (strict tier). Cohorts can share a brain bank or protocol, so the count of cohorts is not a count of independent confirmations. A cell type without a hit is not shown as a negative result: with 2 cohorts the test has little power (p-value floor).

Genes and strict

The number of genes that replicate and meet the effect filter in that cell type, and how many of them also pass the strict minimum-effect test. The examples are the strongest genes by r-value, split by direction.

{% call section_help('Replicated genes', 'vwf-p2-help') %}

The genes of the main cell type that replicate and meet the effect filter, best r-value first. Direction and effect are the median over the cohorts that carry the result (log2 fold change; per Braak stage or per decade for the trend facets). Cohorts significant counts the cohorts with q below 0.05 on their own in the same direction, of those tested. Carried by names the two cohorts that give the r-value. The star marks the strict tier. Flags mark gene classes that move for technical reasons (long genes, lncRNAs, sex chromosome, ribosomal and mitochondrial genes, the APOE locus).

The list holds the first 500 replicated genes by r-value; the counts above are not capped. Click a gene to open its page with the cohorts behind the result.

{% call section_help('Gene sets', 'vwf-p3-help') %}

Gene sets whose mean score per donor follows {{ facet_label }}: the same partial conjunction rule as for genes, on permutation p values of the mean module score, Benjamini-Hochberg over the modules of one family. Pseudobulk of the member genes is used where counts exist, else the mean of per-cell scores. The effect filter for sets is a standardised effect of at least 0.2 standard deviations in 2 cohorts; there is no strict tier.

{% endcall %}
