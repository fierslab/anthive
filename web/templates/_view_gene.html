What shifts it: facet effects panel 2, tested per donor or mouse

Exploratory. Effects are tested per donor (per mouse in the xenograft block) within one cell type. Click a cell to see the cohorts behind it.

A change of a whole cell type can be a shift in the mix of cell states inside it (composition), not induction of the gene in every cell. Braak stage and pathologic AD are one pathology axis, so where both replicate it is one finding.
{% call doc_block('About this view', id='vwg-about') %}

A facet is a donor-level property such as sex, Braak stage or age, defined the same way in every dataset. A facet effect is a difference between donors (between host mice in the xenograft block), never between single cells.

A cell is replicated when the same direction shows in at least 2 cohorts according to a partial conjunction test at 5 percent FDR (r-value below 0.05). The effect filter (at least a 10 percent change in 2 cohorts, 10 percent over three stages for Braak, per decade for age) is shown separately as a condition on the effect, not as error control. The star marks the strict tier: the minimum-effect r-value is also below 0.05 (strict tier). No evidence shows the cohorts that are individually significant out of those tested (k/m); it does not mean there is no effect, because few or small cohorts cannot reach one (p-value floor).

The post-mortem and the xenograft system are analysed separately and never combined (scopes). Cohorts can share a brain bank, protocol or lab, so "cohorts" is not a count of independent confirmations.
